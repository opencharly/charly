# PLACEHOLDER — the go-skill environment section, made runnable by a cold reader

Executes the batch named when `#310` landed. All six members, plus the sixth that the `#310`
validator asked be added and explicitly asked NOT be pushed as a commit on that PR — doing it
here is what the batch was for.

Every finding came from the same instrument: a reader given only the section, forbidden every
other source, asked to perform the procedure. Each defect is one the prose was *true* about and
still could not be followed.

## The recipe now has a worked example, and it is a real one

`CHARLY_PLUGIN_ONLY=1 ./bin/charly <word>` had no usable `<word>`. The section's only concrete
word, `examplecommand`, is explicitly disqualified two paragraphs above as NOT baked — so the one
example a reader could reach was the one the procedure does not apply to.

Now worked with `generate-packages`, which is baked by the package AND declared by
`candy/generate-packages/`, with both precondition checks published as runnable commands and the
arms published with the `mv`/restore that makes them executable.

**The first revision of this entry pasted a rendering and called it verified.** Both arm outputs
were shown as one line each. They are not: arm 1 prints nine lines (rc=1) — a
`Note: local candy … shadows …` preamble, two identical lines per shadowed candy because the
project loads twice — and arm 2 prints 248 lines (rc=80), a usage dump with the documented error
last. The one-line version came from piping through `grep -v '^Note:'`, and was then described as
"verified as published".

**And the fix for that removed the evidence a second fix then relied on.** `tail -1` discards the
preamble — but the preamble IS the discriminator: with the flag charly loads the project and
emits it; without the flag the baked binary short-circuits and emits none, and the last line is
BYTE-IDENTICAL either way. Since `CHARLY_PLUGIN_ONLY` is an exact `1` match, a typo produces a
silently ineffective flag and an identical-looking run.

So the recipe now leads with a control — a predicate on the preamble, no counts, host-independent —
that proves the flag took effect before either arm is read. Two individually-correct fixes had
cancelled each other, and neither was checked against the other.

## Arm 1 gained an observable

Arm 2 gave the reader an exact error string; arm 1 gave nothing to look for. Both arms now show
their output, so the procedure can be checked rather than assumed.

## "Baked" is defined where it becomes load-bearing

The word carried the whole section and was never defined. A word is BAKED when a provider binary
sits in `/usr/lib/charly/plugins/` beside a `<name>.providers` manifest naming it — from the OS
package or a candy's `bake_plugin:` step. Baked-ness is per WORD, not per machine, and
`cat /usr/lib/charly/plugins/*.providers` lists which words are baked on the host in front of you.

## The two `/tmp` rules no longer argue

The section said "run Go builds with `GOTMPDIR=~/.cache/go-tmp`, off the quota-capped temp dir"
and, thirty lines later, "use a short path under `/tmp`". Both true, both about a temp dir, and
read together they contradict.

They are different variables for different reasons, and that is now stated: `GOTMPDIR` carries
Go's BUILD WORK, which is large, so it moves off the quota-capped dir and may be long — nothing
creates a socket there. `$TMPDIR` carries the plugin SOCKET, which is a few bytes, so the quota is
irrelevant and the only constraint is path LENGTH. Short-and-on-`/tmp` for one,
long-and-off-`/tmp` for the other.

## The budget carries its unit

"leaving `$TMPDIR` a little under 90" sat beside an exact "107 bytes" cap. Now "a little under 90
bytes once the `/plugin<digits>` suffix is accounted for".

## One frame, not two

The `#310` validator's residual: "reaches the project-built plugin" (ground truth about what
served the call) sat adjacent to "shows the word resolves but not WHAT served it" (what the
reader may conclude). Both true; together they read as a contradiction. The passage now stays in
the reader's frame throughout — arm 1 tells you it resolved, arm 2 tells you what served it, the
pair licenses the conclusion.

## Two clauses deleted, and that is the whole of the final round

The third review round found defects only inside text the second round's fixes added. That is the
agreed signal to stop explaining and start deleting, so both fixes are deletions:

- **"both the set and the ordering vary by host and by run"** overshot. Measured across repeated
  runs: **one** distinct set every time, **two** distinct orderings. The set varies by HOST; the
  ordering varies by run. The instruction it supports — use a predicate, do not count — is
  unaffected, which is why the clause could simply go.
- **"a typo fails closed"** was the wrong term for a search that gets WIDER. A typo leaves the FHS
  path in place: safe for a deployed container, and a silently ineffective flag for the test. The
  text now says both, and names that asymmetry as the reason the procedure leads with a control.
  Pre-existing wording, exposed by this cutover's own new text rather than introduced by it.
